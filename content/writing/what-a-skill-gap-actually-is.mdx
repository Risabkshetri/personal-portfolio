"Skill-gap analysis" usually means: take a target role, take a person, diff the
skill lists, return what's missing. That diff is almost useless. It's long, it's
generic, and it treats every missing skill as equally worth acquiring.

A more useful definition: **a skill-gap is the ordered set of skills where
acquiring each one measurably changes the person's odds of the outcome they
want.** Two claims are doing work there.

**Ordered.** Most of the value is in the first two or three items. A ranking that
puts "system design" above "learn a second cloud provider" for a given profile is
worth more than a complete unranked list. The ranking is the product; the list is
a commodity.

**Outcome-linked.** "Missing relative to a job description" and "missing relative
to people who actually got this job from a similar starting point" are different
diffs, and the second one is the one that matters. Job descriptions are wish
lists. Hiring outcomes are ground truth where you can get them.

This reframing has consequences for how you build the thing:

- You need outcome data (who moved from state A to state B), not just a skill
  taxonomy.
- Cold-start profiles (a first-year with no history) will get a weak, wide read,
  and the honest move is to show that uncertainty rather than hide it.
- The output should be "these three things, in this order, and here is why"
  rather than a score or a syllabus.
