I came to India with one goal: to build a company.

I was always curious about technology, new innovations, and what could be built
with them. Before starting my B.Tech, I had already learned website
development and the fundamentals of programming.

Friends often asked me, "How do you know all this? What should I learn next?"

That question eventually became the starting point of Zobique.

I wanted to build an intelligent system that could guide students, help them
understand their potential, and act like a personal career assistant.

I brought two talented classmates, Mohit Kholiya and Vansh Attri, on board as
co-founders.

## From college nights to government interest

We spent nights building algorithms and multi-agent systems while managing
college assignments during the day.

Six months in, Vansh decided that the startup journey was not for him. Six
months later, Mohit made the same decision.

Meanwhile, our product was gaining traction.

The National Career Service under the Ministry of Labour and Employment
showed interest in integrating our algorithm into its existing portal. With
support from Dr. Umesh Dutta, we got the opportunity to present our work to
government officials.

The integration had to be ready within a week.

We delivered in four days what we believed would take a month. [How the
matching engine actually works](/writing/ncs-matching-engine-in-depth) is its
own piece; this one is about what we got wrong on the business side.

This was before coding agents became reliable enough for everyday
engineering. We built complex systems from scratch, learned by doing, and
shipped under pressure.

Today, AI coding agents make engineers like us significantly faster. Instead
of hiring interns for every repetitive task, we started using agents to
build, research, and execute.

But we learned something more important than how to build software.

## Our biggest mistake was not technical

We ran pilots with [over 1,500 students](/writing/aggregate-skill-gap-patterns).
More than 50 students got placed through the ecosystem we were building.

Yet revenue remained a problem.

We had built the product, worked with institutions, and demonstrated
technical capability. But we had neglected marketing, positioning, and brand
building.

People did not know what Zobique was, who it served, or why they should pay
for it.

Traction was not translating into a sustainable business.

Mohit eventually decided to move on as well.

I had come to India to build a company, not simply to earn a degree or
secure a placement. So I continued.

## Building Zobique Labs differently

I realized that founder survival comes first.

We shifted our focus towards [Zobique Labs](https://labs.zobique.com),
providing technology solutions to businesses while using the revenue to fund
research and product development.

We are not trying to be another traditional software agency that simply
executes a statement of work and moves on.

We focus on the problem, what we ship, and the outcome it creates.

Today, we manage a team of five, work on real business problems, and
continue exploring ideas that can become scalable products.

Some of the systems we have researched and built include:

- **[Lead-qualification agent](/deployments/lead-qualification-automation):**
  Automating business research and lead qualification.
- **[Voicenity](/deployments/ai-telecalling-system):** A cost-effective voice
  AI solution designed to support more than 40 languages.
- **[Unified CMS](/writing/multi-tenant-headless-cms-architecture):**
  Managing content across multiple websites from one dashboard.

Client work gives us exposure to real problems. Revenue gives us the
resources to experiment. R&D helps us turn what we learn into products.

That is the model we are building around.

## What I am building towards

I still love building technology. That has not changed.

What has changed is how I think about building a company.

A great product is not enough. Technical execution is not enough. Even real
traction is not enough if you cannot turn it into a sustainable business.

You need distribution, positioning, customer trust, and a clear reason for
people to buy.

I am still learning these things while building [Zobique
Labs](https://labs.zobique.com).

The goal is simple: solve real problems, ship useful technology, and build
products that businesses genuinely need.

**We know how to build. Now, we are learning how to build a business around
it.**
