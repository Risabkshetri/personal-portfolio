## The constraint

Bizowl is building a startup operating system for India: one place where
founders can find services, people and resources. Its first product was built
on React, Node.js and Firebase, and it did the job for a while. Then the idea
grew faster than the code did.

Adding anything meant fighting the structure, because there was not much
structure to work with. And the product had a second problem, one that no
amount of refactoring fixes: a new user could land on it and not be sure what
Bizowl was for or what to do next.

I do not think of a rebuild as a technical problem first. The first job was to
sit with the founder and understand what Bizowl was meant to become, because a
platform rebuilt around the wrong idea is just a cleaner version of the wrong
thing.

## What I built

We moved Bizowl onto a modular platform: a set of reusable core services, so
that new parts of the product plug in instead of being forced in. I chose the
stack for how well it would hold up over years of change, not for how quickly it
would demo.

On top of that foundation I built the marketplace module, and gave it a clear
job inside the wider Bizowl ecosystem. I redesigned the navigation around one
question, "where am I and where can I go?", and migrated the existing client
and partner data so nobody had to start again.

The order mattered: understand the vision, design the foundation, ship the
marketplace, then migrate the data. Migrating first would have meant moving data
into a structure we had not finished deciding.

## Outcome

The marketplace module is live on the new platform, the existing data is
migrated, and the experience is clearer for the people using it. I do not have
numbers to put next to that, and I would rather say so than invent them.
