## The constraint

First-contact telecalling for real-estate leads is repetitive, high-volume, and
mostly unproductive per call. The small fraction of interested leads still
matters a lot. Paying trained people to spend most of their day on "no answer"
and "not interested" is expensive, and call quality drifts by the afternoon.

## What I built

A voice agent that handles the first call:

- Places the call, runs a short qualification script, and adapts to the replies.
- For interested leads: captures requirements and books a follow-up on a real
  human's calendar.
- For everyone else: logs the outcome and a transcript summary so a person can
  review the edge cases.

Source is public: [`Risabkshetri/AI-Telecalling`](https://github.com/Risabkshetri/AI-Telecalling).

## What broke

- **Latency kills a phone call.** Response gaps that are fine in chat feel broken
  on a call. Getting time-to-first-audio under control mattered more than model
  quality.
- **Interruptions and hold music.** Real calls have people talking over the
  agent, IVR menus, and dead air. The happy-path script was the easy 20%.
- **Knowing when to give up.** The agent needed a firm rule for ending a call
  gracefully rather than looping when a lead was clearly disengaged.
